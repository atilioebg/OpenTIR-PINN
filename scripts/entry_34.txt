### 1. Conceptual Rationale: Why Direct Dashboard Grounding Matters
In high-impact scientific peer review (Q1 / IEEE TGRS / RSE), reviewers assessing synthetic datasets frequently raise two skepticism barriers:
1. *"How were these synthetic forward radiances generated, and were realistic atmospheric models and Planck blackbody temperatures used?"*
2. *"What does each partition represent, and how do we know the network didn't cheat through data leakage or cross-fold contamination?"*

Previously, answering these questions required the reviewer to navigate away from the interactive visualization and cross-reference multiple markdown documentation files (`DATA_DICTIONARY.md`, `README.md`). By embedding an in-situ expandable scientific dossier (`st.expander`) directly into Tab 6 (`Forward Datasets & Sample-Level Inspector`), the reviewer obtains instantaneous, self-contained mathematical and physical clarity without disrupting the interactive observation inspector.

### 2. Physical & Architectural Breakdown of the 4 Quadrants

#### The 2×2 Generalization Matrix System
Thermal infrared remote sensing inversion models are notoriously vulnerable to learning shortcut correlations between ground emissivity spectra and atmospheric absorption lines. To formally separate material transferability from atmospheric transferability, the benchmark spans an orthogonal matrix across two independent axes:
* **Axis 1: Surface Material Distribution** (Seen 233 Development Pool vs. Unseen 7 Immutable Golden Targets).
* **Axis 2: Atmospheric Column Distribution** (Seen Nominal Development Atmospheres vs. Unseen Polar Winter Atmospheres).

```
                      [ Atmospheric Distribution Axis ]
                      In-Distribution (Nominal)     OOD (Severe Polar Winter)
                   +-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
   In-Distribution |         QUADRANT I          |        QUADRANT III         |
   (Dev Pool 233)  |  • Master Training: 5,500   |  • LOCO Generalization      |
                   |  • Out-of-Fold Val: 2,024   |  • Tab 5 Dynamic RTE Studio |
                   +-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
   Out-of-Dist.    |         QUADRANT II         |        QUADRANT IV          |
   (Golden Targets)|  • Pure Spectral OOD: 308   |  • Joint Extreme OOD: 770   |
   (7 Immutable)   |  • Zero-Shot Material Eval  |  • Double-Blind Stress Test |
                   +-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
```

#### Detailed Breakdown of Each Partition:
1. **🏋️ Quadrant I — Master Training Pool ($N = 5,500$ observations):**
   * **What It Validates:** In-distribution empirical optimization. Proves the PINN can simultaneously learn Planck thermodynamic scaling and multi-task loss without overfitting or gradient instability.
   * **Exact Values & Composition:** 500 spectral profiles sampled across the 233 development materials, coupled across 4 nominal MODTRAN 5.2 atmospheres (Tropical, Mid-Latitude Summer, Sub-Arctic Summer, US Standard 1976) across 11 discrete kinetic surface temperatures ($T_s \in [280, 330]\,\mathrm{K}$ in $5.0\,\mathrm{K}$ increments).
2. **🧪 Quadrant I — Out-of-Fold Validation ($N = 2,024$ observations):**
   * **What It Validates:** Leak-free in-distribution generalization. Validates the model's ability to invert seen mineral classes with unseen sample preparations under standard weather.
   * **Exact Values & Composition:** 46 unseen development spectra isolated strictly in Fold 0 via `StratifiedGroupKFold(n_splits=5)` grouped by specimen `sample_id` (guaranteeing 0% intra-sample leakage), coupled across the 4 nominal atmospheres at the 11 temperatures ($46 \times 4 \times 11 = 2,024$).
3. **🏆 Quadrant II — Golden Target Benchmark ($N = 308$ observations):**
   * **What It Validates:** **Zero-Shot Material Generalization.** Proves the network can disentangle temperature and emissivity on completely novel geological, urban, and vegetative surfaces with sharp Reststrahlen features never presented during training, under benign atmospheric conditions.
   * **Exact Values & Composition:** 7 Golden Targets (`quartz`, `calcite`, `kaolinite`, `feldspar`, `urban_concrete`, `urban_asphalt`, `npv_bark`) coupled across 4 nominal atmospheres at 11 temperatures ($7 \times 4 \times 11 = 308$).
4. **❄️ Quadrant III — Atmospheric OOD Stress Test:**
   * **What It Validates:** Pure atmospheric transferability. Evaluates network resilience against extreme dry atmospheres, polar vortex conditions, and high path transmittance independently of material variations. Evaluated dynamically via Leave-One-Cluster-Out (LOCO) benchmarking and accessible interactively in Tab 5 (Dynamic RTE Studio).
5. **❄️ Quadrant IV — Double-Blind Golden Benchmark ($N = 770$ observations):**
   * **What It Validates:** **The Extreme Joint Domain Shift.** Evaluates whether the PINN retains physical fidelity when *both* the surface material AND the atmospheric profile are outside the training envelope. Provides the key empirical proof showing a $-79.3\%$ error reduction over NASA's classical TES algorithm.
   * **Exact Values & Composition:** 7 Golden Targets coupled exclusively with 10 severe sub-arctic and mid-latitude winter MODTRAN LUTs (quarantined Model 3 & Model 5 across water vapor scales $0.50\times$ to $2.00\times$, $\mathrm{PWV} \in [0.21, 1.70]\,\mathrm{g/cm}^2$) across all 11 temperatures ($7 \times 10 \times 11 = 770$).

### 3. Forward Radiative Transfer Synthesis & Exact Simulation Parameters
* **Boundary-Value Radiative Transfer Equation:**
  $$L_{\mathrm{sensor}}(\lambda) = \tau(\lambda) \left[ \varepsilon(\lambda) B(\lambda, T_s) + (1 - \varepsilon(\lambda)) L^\downarrow(\lambda) \right] + L^\uparrow(\lambda)$$
* **Sensor Convolutions:**
  * **ASTER (5 Discrete Bands):** $8.125\text{--}11.650\,\mu\mathrm{m}$, convolved using JPL laboratory Spectral Response Functions.
  * **HyTES (256 Hyperspectral Channels):** $7.49\text{--}12.00\,\mu\mathrm{m}$, $\Delta\lambda \approx 17.5\,\mathrm{nm}$, Gaussian instrument SRF ($\text{FWHM} \approx 17.7\,\mathrm{nm}$).
* **Kinetic Surface Temperatures:** 11 levels uniformly sampled from $280.0\,\mathrm{K}$ ($6.85\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$) to $330.0\,\mathrm{K}$ ($56.85\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$) in $5.0\,\mathrm{K}$ increments.
* **Corpus Totals:** 8,602 forward coupled samples per sensor architecture ($5,500 + 2,024 + 308 + 770$), yielding $17,204$ coupled radiative observations across ASTER and HyTES.
